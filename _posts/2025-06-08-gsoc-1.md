With the coding period (and [swotvac](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/swot_vac)) officially kicking off this week, I've been able to devote more time to GSoC, and have spent it getting around the logistics of the first issue on my [to-do list](https://github.com/pymc-devs/pymc-extras/issues/340): [Implement a Newton solver](https://github.com/pymc-devs/pymc-extras/issues/342).

In short, a key step of [INLA](https://www.precision-analytics.ca/articles/a-gentle-inla-tutorial/) (the "LA" to be exact) involves estimating integrals of the form below using what's known as the Laplace approximation:

\begin{equation}
\int e^{f(x)} dx \approx e^{f(x_0)}(2 \pi)^{n/2}\sqrt{|-Hf(x_0)|}
\end{equation}

Where $$Hf(x)$$ is the Hessian of some multivariate scalar function f and $$x_0$$ is a stationary point. Note the similarity between the RHS and the negative log likelihood of a Gaussian. In our case, this stationary point happens to be the mode of our distribution, which we need to find. Our Newton solver should do just that!
